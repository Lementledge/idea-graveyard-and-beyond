# Contributing Guidelines

Thank you for your interest in exploring, critiquing, or expanding upon these ideas. 

Because the entries in this repository represent theoretical concepts, unverified hypotheses, and speculative architectures that currently exceed personal resources or scope, community contributions look different here than in a standard software repository.

---

## How You Can Contribute

### 1. Constructive Critique & Refutation
Rigorous counter-arguments are just as valuable as validations. If you identify:
- A mathematical inconsistency or logical flaw
- A physical constraint, computational boundary, or existing no-go theorem that invalidates an assumption
- An overlooked edge case or contradiction

Please open a **GitHub Discussion** under the **"Refutations & Critiques"** category or submit an Issue detailing why the premise fails.

### 2. Prior Art & Citations
If an idea described here has already been explored, proven, or refuted in academic literature:
- Submit a Pull Request adding the relevant papers/preprints to the `References` section of that idea's markdown file.
- Provide a brief summary of how the existing work aligns with or diverges from the proposal.

### 3. Formalization & Implementation (Toy Models)
If you wish to test an idea by building a proof-of-concept, running simulations, or proving lemmas:
- Feel free to fork the repository and work on your own implementation.
- You may submit a PR adding links to your external implementations/benchmarks in the idea's entry.

### 4. Proposing New Ideas
External ideas are welcome, provided they meet the repository's baseline standards:
- Follow `templates/idea-template.md` strictly.
- State clearly **what makes the idea bottlenecked** (e.g., lack of computational resources, domain expertise, or experimental apparatus).
- Avoid vague brainstorming; proposals should formulate specific mechanics, assumptions, and testable questions.

---

## Where to Engage

- **GitHub Discussions**: Preferred for open-ended debates, thought experiments, alternative interpretations, and theoretical inquiries.
- **GitHub Issues**: Reserved for specific factual errors, broken links, structural fixes, or direct mathematical/logical disproofs.
- **Pull Requests**: For documentation fixes, adding relevant citations, updating progress status, or submitting new well-structured entries.

---

## Attribution & Intellectual Property

By contributing to this repository, you agree that your contributions will be licensed under the repository's open license. If you develop an independent project or publication based on an idea here, attribution is appreciated though not strictly restrictive.
